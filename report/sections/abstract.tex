AI coding agents no longer only write code: they open, trigger and appear in GitHub issues, yet empirical studies of these agents have focused on their pull requests. This paper presents, to the researcher's knowledge, the first comparison of the issues attributed to many agents. Following the Goal--Question--Metric approach, it analyses \NIssues{} issues from \NRepos{} repositories attributed to \NAgents{} agents, combining non-parametric tests with effect sizes, survival analysis that accounts for issues still open, and topic models checked for stability across runs and confirmed with a second method. The attribution, the topic labels and the language flag were also validated by hand on blind random samples. The agents differ most in how an issue is linked to them. For CodeRabbit and Devin, most issues were written by the agent's own bot, and these are closed 18 to 24 percentage points less often than the same agents' other issues, a gap that issue age does not explain. Time-to-close differs across agents with a medium effect, but rankings based on closed issues alone disagree with the survival analysis. Topics also depend on the agent, most clearly for CodeRabbit's review follow-ups. The manual check found \ValCorrectPct\% of attributions correct, with the agent only mentioned in half of the correct ones. The label ``AI agent'' therefore covers distinct kinds of work, and studies that compare agents should separate the agent's role, measure speed with survival analysis, and validate attributions and topics by hand.
